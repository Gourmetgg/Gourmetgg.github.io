notes before conclusions

writing while the question is still interesting

02 June 20262 min read[ learning, essays ]

It is tempting to wait until an idea is finished before writing it down. Finished ideas are easier to present. Their rough edges have been sanded away; their questions have become statements.

But the unfinished version often contains the most useful part: a record of how the thinking happened.

keep the uncertainty

A notebook can say “I do not understand this yet.” It can hold two explanations that disagree. It can include a small example that fails for reasons you have not found.

Writing those things down is not a failure to explain. It is a way to make the next step visible. “This does not work” becomes “this works until the input is empty.” That is already progress.

A note does not have to settle a question. Sometimes its job is to give the question a better shape.

something to return to

The other benefit is memory. When you return a month later, you do not have to reconstruct the entire investigation. You have left a trail.

This is the kind of writing I want to practice here: specific enough to be useful, honest enough to remain provisional. A place for explanations, but also for the notes that come before them.
