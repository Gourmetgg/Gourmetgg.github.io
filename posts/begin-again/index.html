permission to begin again

unfinished projects are not the whole story

03 April 20241 min read[ essays, life ]

There is a folder on almost every computer I have used that contains beginnings. A few paragraphs, a half-built page, a program that worked once. They are easy to read as evidence of something lacking.

But a beginning can teach you something even when it does not become a finished thing.

what remains

You may learn that the question was different from the one you thought you were asking. You may learn a tool. You may find that the project needs more time than you can give it right now.

None of this makes finishing irrelevant. It just means an unfinished project is not an empty one.

make the next step smaller

When I return to something, I try not to begin with the entire plan. I look for the next concrete action: read the last paragraph, run the program, write down what is missing.

A small action is easier to do than a renewed promise. It also tells you more. After ten minutes, you might know whether the project still interests you.

And if it does, you can begin again without pretending that the first beginning never happened.
