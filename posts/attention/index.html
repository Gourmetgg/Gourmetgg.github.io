where does the afternoon go?

on attention, open tabs, and unfinished tea

21 September 20262 min read[ life, essays ]

There are afternoons that disappear without leaving much evidence. A cup of tea goes cold. A dozen tabs accumulate. The light on the wall moves from one side of the room to the other.

I used to call these unproductive afternoons. Now I am less sure that productivity is the right measure. Sometimes I have been resting. Sometimes I have been avoiding something. The two can look very similar from the outside.

a smaller question

Instead of asking whether I did enough, I try asking what I actually paid attention to. A conversation? A difficult sentence? A notification that led to another notification?

The answer is usually more useful than a list of completed tasks. Attention is where the day happened, whether or not the calendar recorded it.

One small practice helps: before opening a new tab, write down the question it is meant to answer. If there is no question, perhaps there is no need for the tab.

nothing to optimize

This is not an argument for making every minute purposeful. A walk without a destination is a good use of an afternoon. So is reading something that has no practical application.

I just want to notice the difference between choosing to wander and being carried along. The tea is still going to go cold sometimes. I would like to remember what I was thinking about when it did.
