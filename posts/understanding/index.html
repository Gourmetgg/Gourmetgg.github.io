the difference between knowing and explaining

an explanation is a useful test of an idea

10 February 20251 min read[ learning, technology ]

I can read an explanation and feel that I understand it. The sentences follow one another comfortably. The conclusion seems reasonable. Then someone asks me to explain it, and the confidence disappears.

Recognition and understanding overlap, but they are not the same thing.

start with an example

When an idea is slippery, I find a small example. Not the most general case. Not the most elegant one. Something I can work through without skipping a step.

For a sorting algorithm, that might mean five numbers on paper. For a cache, it might mean two requests and a value that changes between them.

The example gives the explanation somewhere to stand. If I cannot explain what happens there, a more abstract vocabulary will probably not help.

notice the missing step

Writing an explanation makes gaps visible. “Obviously” is often the place where I have stopped thinking. “It just works” is often the part I should inspect.

A useful explanation does not need to sound sophisticated. It needs to let another person follow the path. If that path has a missing step, I have found something worth learning.
